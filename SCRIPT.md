# The Emu War — narration v1

**Provisional duration:** 60.80 s at 3.75 words/s (228 words). Real timing must replace this after voice generation.

## Narration

In 1932, the Australian army went to war with twenty thousand emus. The emus won.
Western Australia. War veterans are farming wheat, and the emus are eating it.
So the farmers ask for machine guns. The minister says yes. He thinks it'll be good target practice.
Three soldiers. Two Lewis guns. Ten thousand bullets.
Day one, they find fifty emus. The farmers try herding them into an ambush, but the emus split into tiny groups.
Two days later, a thousand emus march straight at the guns. The gun jams after twelve birds. The rest scatter.
So the major bolts a gun to a truck. The truck can't catch them, and the ride's too bumpy to shoot.
Then they notice every flock has a lookout, a huge black bird, warning the others.
After six days, the army goes home. Parliament jokes the emus deserve the medals. Five days later, it's back.
The major reports almost a thousand emus, for almost ten thousand bullets. Exactly ten bullets per bird.
Wait, how do you lose to a bird?
You see, a machine gun is built for a crowd standing in a line. Emus don't do lines.
Ever tried chasing seagulls off your chips? You charge, the flock splits, and you go in circles.
So Australia sent machine guns after one of the two animals holding up its own coat of arms.
